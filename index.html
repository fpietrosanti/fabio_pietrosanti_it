About

The short version — the same text as on LinkedIn.

I build things that shift power: open-source infrastructure, non-profit ventures and companies — mostly where technology meets civil rights.

I started in information security in 1995, inside the Italian hacker community — a background I never left. In 2005 I co-founded PrivateWave, a mobile voice-encryption company, where I worked with Philip Zimmermann on a ZRTP-based protocol for circuit-switched networks. In 2010 I started GlobaLeaks, free software for secure whistleblowing now used by media, NGOs, public bodies and private companies worldwide, and built the Hermes Center for Transparency and Digital Human Rights around it — today a member of European Digital Rights (EDRi). Since 2016, Whistleblowing Solutions turns that technology into a sustainable service, as a non-profit social enterprise with no private shareholder interests.

In parallel I work on problems that are less political and more physical. At SignalSwarm I lead a fully automated, market-neutral trading platform and the asset management service built on it. For thirteen years, at our family's GLS express-courier branch, I pushed automation, ecological last-mile delivery and cost-control systems into a business that moves parcels for a living. Since 2023 I've been building biohack.it, open infrastructure that turns individual self-experimentation into comparable human data.

What connects them is a method rather than a sector: take something opaque — a supply chain, a market, a bureaucracy, a body — instrument it, and give people the tools to act on what they find. Preferably as free software.

I also help organise the Italian hacker community: the Italian Hacker Camp and the Italian Hacker Embassy at the Dutch and German camps.

Advisory, board seats and partnerships: welcome. Say hello.

02

The long version

Three decades, told through what was built and what it changed.

1995–2004

The hacker scene and the first security jobs

Fabio started in information security in 1995 inside the Italian hacker community, where he is still known as naif. By the late 1990s he was writing in BFi (the s0ftpj e-zine) and contributing to Italian Linux documentation such as Linux Domande e Risposte.

In 2000–2001 he co-founded Sikurezza.org, one of the reference Italian security communities and mailing lists, and was a founding member of the Italian Black Hats, launched at SMAU 2001. In 2002 La Stampa followed him and other Black Hats around Milan testing the security of wireless networks.

Meanwhile the hobby became a profession: CTO of Live Network Security, freelance ICT-security writer for Mondadori's WeekIT and for ICT Security, network security manager at I.NET — where he designed a Security Operation Center — and from 2004 senior information-security consultant reporting to the security CTO of Telecom Italia. Talks from those years cover wireless insecurity (Webbit, SMAU, CNR), anonymous communication (hack.lu 2006) and PGP (e-privacy 2005).

2005–2014

Voice encryption and the honesty of the crypto market

In 2005 he co-founded KHAMSA, later PrivateWave Italia, building mobile voice and messaging encryption (PrivateGSM) for governments and enterprises, and worked with Philip Zimmermann on a ZRTP-based encrypted transport for circuit-switched networks. PrivateWave released ZORG, an open-source C++ and Java ZRTP implementation, in 2011 — the year ZRTP became RFC 6189.

Two international patents came out of that work, filed by KHAMSA Italia with Federico Moro and Lorenzo Boccaccia: WO2008015721 / EP2057808, secure management of multiple identities in centralised messaging services, and WO2008015723 / EP2057777, a method to record a non-repudiable digital communication and identify its participants (see Patents).

On his blog infosecurity.ch he wrote about mobile and voice security and exposed how parts of the market worked: in 2010 he showed that a supposedly independent review of voice-encryption products was traceable to one of the vendors (picked up by The Register), and he wrote against snake-oil claims and marketing “hacking contests”. With others he released OSXCrypt (2008), an attempt to bring TrueCrypt-compatible disk encryption to macOS.

2010–today

Whistleblowing and anonymity infrastructure

In 2010 he started GlobaLeaks, free software for secure and anonymous whistleblowing, and in December 2010 built the Hermes Center for Transparency and Digital Human Rights around it; Hermes has been a member of European Digital Rights (EDRi) since 2018. Hermes also rewrote and maintained Tor2web, the gateway that lets ordinary browsers reach Tor onion services.

GlobaLeaks became the base of whistleblowing platforms run by newsrooms, NGOs and public bodies — from Publeaks in the Netherlands and IRPILeaks to anti-corruption channels with Transparency International Italy and, later, the Italian anti-corruption authority. Since 2016 Whistleblowing Solutions, a non-profit social enterprise, turns that technology into a sustainable service.

He has been a regular in the Tor community since 2011: running exit relays and Tor2web nodes, taking part in the international Tor developers' meetings, and opening tickets that changed Tor itself — OutboundBindAddressExit, shipped in Tor 0.3.0, comes from his 2016 proposal for low-liability exit relays.

2016–2022

Policy, surveillance and democracy

The same method moved into policy. In 2017 he was one of the proponents of the Italian bill on state trojans (captatori informatici) presented by Stefano Quintarelli, and he took the topic to e-privacy, Freedom Not Fear in Brussels and the CCC. He ran the 2017 campaign that pushed VideoLAN to serve VLC over HTTPS, mapped the risks of Kaspersky software in the Italian public sector (Euronews 2018, again in 2022) and used FOIA requests to monitor surveillance.

On electronic voting he wrote the Guida Hermes al voto digitale, analysed the 2017 Lombardy e-voting referendum, animated the CRVD committee, studied how Italians abroad could vote at polling stations (the AIRE study) and, with OpenRousseau, showed the weaknesses of the Five Star Movement's Rousseau platform — including by voting twice on it.

In 2022 Monitora PA, a civic-hacking project run with Hermes and volunteers, pushed thousands of Italian public administrations to remove Google Analytics.

2012–2025

Parcels, logistics and algorithmic finance

For thirteen years he worked in the family's GLS express-courier branch in Latina, bringing automation, measurement systems, cost control and ecological last-mile delivery into a business that moves parcels for a living. That work produced Haulersense (computer vision and revenue recovery for couriers), LESS.green and OpenPUDO — open-source software for pick-up and drop-off networks — and PLogistics, an e-commerce fulfilment operation. In 2022 he taught the logistics of pick-up points at LIUC University.

Since 2019 he has led SignalSwarm in Dubai: a fully automated, market-neutral trading platform and the asset-management service built on it.

2023–today

Biohacking and open human data

Since 2023 he has been building biohack.it with the Hacking Biology Foundation: AGPL-licensed, non-profit infrastructure where self-experimenters document protocols and standard-coded biomarkers, so that individual experiments become comparable human data. He has talked about it on Rai 1 (Codice), Rai Radio 1 (Codice Beta) and at the TimePie biohacker forum in Shanghai.

Truth and accountability

Investigative journalism, whistleblowing and the habit of checking what power says — with the receipts.

Most of this work has one shape: someone claims something — a vendor, a ministry, a platform, a political movement — and the claim is taken on trust because nobody can check it. The answer is always the same: build the tool that makes it checkable, publish the evidence, and let other people verify it themselves.

That is why the software is free and open source, why the evidence sits in public repositories, why the requests to public bodies are filed under freedom-of-information law and published together with their answers, and why every claim on this page links to its source. When the finding was uncomfortable, it was published anyway: in 2020 he showed that the Rousseau platform could not guarantee the integrity of a vote — by voting twice on it. That was activism against the Five Star Movement, against the technological control that a private company, Casaleggio Associati, exercised over an Italian political party. He was expelled for it, and published the expulsion documents.

Concretely: free software that lets newsrooms receive documents from anonymous sources, training for the journalists who use it, transparency requests that force public data into the open, and a handful of investigations run first-hand.
