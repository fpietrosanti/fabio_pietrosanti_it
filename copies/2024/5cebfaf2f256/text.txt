 Abstract

 PDF

 Cryptography-based software and tools have brought new strategies to whistleblowers and to the organisations cooperating or relying on their contributions. The open source software GlobaLeaks, in particular, has become one of the technical choices of reference for journalists and activists interested in launching whistleblowing platforms to attract leaks from anonymous sources. The Italian chapter of NGO Transparency International (TII) has been an early adopter of GlobaLeaks and since 2014 TII has been offering its own platform, ALAC - Allerta Anticorruzione. TII assists Italian whistleblowers, helping them in the process of blowing the whistle and in finding the right recipients for their claims. In Italy, where data about corruption levels is traditionally high and where a proper but limited whistleblowing protection law has been passed in 2017 only, TII has been one of the most vocal actors advocating for whistleblowing and has been involved in a variety of initiatives and actions aiming at building a whistleblowing culture and at fostering effective anti-corruption responses. The aim of this chapter is to analyse ALAC - Allerta Anticorruzione, its rationales, its purposes and strategies focusing on the cooperation between TII and the GlobaLeaks team. Based on qualitative interviews and on an analysis of publicly available documents and reports, this case-study focused chapter will shed light on the ALAC project and on the potentials of digital whistleblowing platforms in the context of anti-corruption purposes and activities.
