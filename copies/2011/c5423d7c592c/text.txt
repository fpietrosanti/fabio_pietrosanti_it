 Egypt's Last Internet Lifeline Put In A Chokehold

 0 comments, 0 called-out 

 + Comment now 

 + Comment now 

 A graph of Noor's connectivity from network monitoring firm Renesys. Click to enlarge.

 When Egypt’s government took the dramatic step of shutting off the entire country’s Internet last Friday amidst nationwide protests, one strand of connectivity remained: the Internet service provider (ISP) Noor, which serves many of the country’s corporations, including its banks.

 As of Monday evening, that remaining connection to the Internet seems to have been mostly severed. “It looks like our last terrestrial hope has been shut down,” writes Jacob Appelbaum, a spokesperson for the anonymity and anti-censorship organization Tor in his Twitter feed. “my connections to systems on Noor are all down.”

 “Large sections of Noor appear to be unreachable and this seems to include DSL services in Cairo,” Appelbaum added . 

 Fabio Pietrosanti, another cybersecurity researcher and chief technology officer for security firm PrivateWave, confirmed in an interview that Egypt’s last ISP had dropped off the Internet. “When the Egypt stuff started, I began monitoring some of the netblocks,” he said, referring to a group of IP addresses, in an interview. “They’re currently not reachable. They were definitely reachable Saturday.”

 In a blog post earlier Monday, Internet monitoring firm Renesys speculated on why Noor, which accounted for about 8% of Egypt’s data traffic, had maintained its connection to the Internet while other ISPs including Link Egypt, Vodafone/Raya, Telecom Egypt, Etisalat Misr, were all taken offline. The most likely reason: Noor hosts Egypt’s banking system, as well as major corporations like Egypt Air and Exxon Mobile. Whether those corporations lost connectivity along with the rest of Noor’s users isn’t clear.

 But Internet monitoring firm Rensys updated its blog post around 4pm Eastern U.S. time. “As of approximately 20:46 UTC, four hours after this blog was first published, Noor started disappearing from the Internet,” the blog states. “They are completely unavailable at present.”
