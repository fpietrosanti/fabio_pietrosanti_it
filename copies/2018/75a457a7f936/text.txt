--- 2018-01-14T20:49:29Z konklone
I agree that this would be useful -- but I think there are two big issues here compared to something like SSL Labs:

* Maintenance over time. One of the things that makes SSL Labs so powerful is that it raises its standards each year as the world changes, while always keeping an A/A+ an achievable grade. 

* Pshtt only looks at a few things, really -- it's an HTTP/HTTPS/HSTS behavior analyzer. It doesn't look at other aspects of TLS connection security like sslyze or SSL Labs do. SSL Labs, in particular, does a very thorough job of attempting/simulating connections from various client profiles, particularly needed with different levels of cipher/protocol support out there, and different root stores.

Just my opinion, but I think given pshtt's limited scope, it's unlikely we could give a grade that would be a useful/living "stopping point" for people looking to tighten up their HTTPS security.
