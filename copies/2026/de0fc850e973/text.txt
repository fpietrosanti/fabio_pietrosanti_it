 Advisory Board
 Eleven experts contributing to the strategic development of the Digital Commons EDIC.

 The Advisory Board brings together eleven experts to provide strategic and technical advice to the Assembly of Members of the Digital Commons EDIC. Their combined experience spans software deployment and design, security, communities, maintenance, law and governance, digital autonomy, open-source AI, funding and entrepreneurship.

 The Assembly appointed the members on 16 October 2026 for a two-year term, ending on 15 October 2028. The Board elects its Chair from among its members.

 Thierry Fraudet
 Thierry Fraudet serves as Executive Advisor to the CDIO, Senior Fellow for IT architecture and Software Engineering, and Open Source Program Office (OSPO) Lead at Michelin. His work focuses on developing long-term technology roadmaps, aligning digital transformation with business strategy, and fostering innovative thinking across the organization. Active in the broader enterprise architecture community, he also serves on the Board of Directors for Club Urba-EA. Drawing on his background in open-source governance and strategic technology evolution, Thierry provides technical leadership to help continuously modernise complex IT landscapes.

 Lea Gimpel
 Lea Gimpel is Director of Policy & AI Lead at the Digital Public Goods Alliance (DPGA) and an internationally recognised expert in open technology policy, public AI, and digital commons management. She specialises in bridging public institutions, technical communities, and the open movement globally. Previously, she co-led an open-source AI language initiative at GIZ. Lea combines technical and legal expertise with deep policy experience across global institutions, having advised the UN, the German UNESCO Commission, and the German Federal Ministry for the Environment. She serves on the board of the Open Knowledge Foundation Germany and holds an Executive MPA in Digital Governance from the Hertie School, where she has lectured on technology policy.

 Giacomo Grassi
 Giacomo Grassi is Director of User Experience & Digital Process at INPS, the Italian National Social Security Institute, where he leads user-centred digital transformation for an ecosystem serving more than 45 million people. His work focuses on service design, design systems, experience governance, language simplification, user research and the human-centred adoption of AI in the public sector. He is also the founder and director of Lo Stato del Design, a conference and community created to strengthen the role of design as a driver for the transformation of public administration. His work has received several international recognitions, including the Red Dot Design Award, iF Design Award and a Compasso d’Oro ADI Honourable Mention. He is the author of Brand e User Experience and Innovare nella Pubblica Amministrazione.

 Franziska Heine
 Franziska Heine studied Media Design in Weimar and began her career in research before moving into the digital economy, including roles at eBay, Microsoft and Nokia. She joined Wikimedia Deutschland in 2017 as Head of Software Development, leading interdisciplinary teams on key projects such as Wikidata. She has co-led Wikimedia Deutschland since 2022 and has served as its sole CEO since April 2025, shaping its mission to advance open knowledge and digital infrastructure for the public good.

 Piotr P. Karwasz
 Piotr P. Karwasz maintains Apache Log4j and Apache Commons, two of the most widely deployed open source Java libraries, and serves on the Apache Software Foundation's Security Team, where he handles vulnerability disclosure across the Foundation's projects. As the Foundation's Vice President for ECMA Relations he represents it in Ecma International's TC-54, which develops the standards for software supply chain transparency. His work has been funded at different times by the Sovereign Tech Agency, the GitHub Secure Open Source Fund and the OpenSSF Alpha-Omega project, which has given him a practitioner's view of how critical open source infrastructure is actually financed. He came to software from mathematics. A graduate of the École normale supérieure in Paris, with a doctorate in algebraic geometry, he taught for over a decade at the University of Gdańsk before moving into IT full time. He runs Copernik, an independent open source and security consultancy, from Gdynia, Poland.

 Amandine Le Pape
 Amandine Le Pape is a co-founder and Guardian of the Matrix.org Foundation. The Foundation is the neutral custodian for the Matrix open standard, aiming to democratise secure online communication and solve the problem of fragmentation in current Chat, VoIP, VR and IoT technologies. Amandine is also COO and co-founder for Element, the company building the eponymous open-source, secure and interoperable collaboration app built on Matrix. Element delivers Matrix solutions to millions of users, including the German Armed Forces, the Swedish and French governments, the UN, and private companies like Mozilla or Red Hat. In addition, Amandine is a founding member of the European Open Source Academy where she heads the Business and Impact section.

 Michiel Leenaars
 Michiel Leenaars is director of strategy at NLnet Foundation, and chair of Edsger Institute and The Commons Conservancy. He leads several international R&D programmes on digital commons, such as NGI Zero Commons Fund, Restack, Rebase and GeoCommons. In addition, Leenaars is active on the board of several organisations, such as the e-democracy initiative Petities.nl, Technology Commons Trust and OpenDoc Society. Previously he was co-editor of the first two European e-Infrastructure Roadmaps, chair of the Netherlands Education Council, member of the strategic committee of the European registry EURId, W3C liaison officer at W3C Benelux and coordinator of the national software engineering research at the Netherlands Research Council. In 2022 he received the NLUUG Lifetime Achievement Award.

 Catharina Maracke
 Catharina Maracke is a senior legal and policy expert with more than 20 years of leadership in intellectual property and Open Source governance. She began her career directing Creative Commons’ global license porting project and later founded the Software Compliance Academy to support organizations in their Open Source journey. Currently serving as VP Legal and Public Policy at the Eclipse Foundation, she also brings experience from industry and the World Economic Forum where she served on the Council for the Future of the Intellectual Property System. She is also a Project Professor at the Cyber Civilization Research Center at Keio University in Japan, alongside past academic appointments at leading universities worldwide. Elected to the Open Source Initiative’s board in 2021, she served as chair from 2022 to 2024.

 François Pellegrini
 François Pellegrini is an open-source strategist and consultant. He co-created the Libre Software Meeting in 2000, and chairs NAOS Cluster, a non-profit which promotes entrepreneurial projects using open license business models in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region. He is the coauthor, with Sébastien Canevet, of the reference book "Droit des Logiciels - Logiciels privatifs et logiciels libres". He is a professor of informatics at Université de Bordeaux, where he also teaches digital law. His research activities at LaBRI and Inria focus on high-performance parallel computing, software law and personal data law. He is a former vice-president of the French Data Protection Authority (CNIL).

 Fabio Pietrosanti
 Fabio Pietrosanti has worked with free software technologies since 1995. He promotes policies and practical software reuse in the private and public sectors, bridging the gap between legal and technical domains.

 Paolo Vecchi
 Paolo Vecchi has been working in the IT industry for the past 40 years in technical and managerial positions. During the past 26 years he has focused on promoting and delivering Open Source software to the private and public sectors.
