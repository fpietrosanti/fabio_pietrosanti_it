 The Digital Commons EDIC adopts joint strategy to scale open-source alternatives
 A joint strategy for 2026–2027, new European partners and an eleven-member Advisory Board.

 The Assembly of Members adopts a 2026–2027 strategy to make open-source tools easier to deploy and sustain, approves Poland’s accession as a Member State, welcomes Croatia and Norway as Observers and appoints an Advisory Board. 

 At its meeting on 16 October 2026, the Assembly of Members of the Digital Commons EDIC adopted its Implementation Strategy for 2026–2027.

 Organisations across Europe often tackle similar integration, procurement and maintenance challenges separately. The strategy brings countries, businesses and open-source communities together to share expertise, reduce duplicated effort and make successful deployments easier to repeat.

 Implementation Strategy

 The aim is to make open-source alternatives dependable choices for everyday work, building on digital commons: software and open standards that public institutions, companies and communities develop and govern together, keeping them available for others to use and adapt.

 It aligns with the EU Open Source Strategy , linking adoption and long-term maintenance of open source to Europe’s technological sovereignty, resilience and competitiveness.

 Three complementary priorities guide the work:

 Connect: help public administrations, businesses and open-source communities find the right expertise, partners and support. A common entry point will connect needs and contribution offers with available expertise and existing services. Practical guidance will help organisations navigate procurement, migration and deployment.

 Deploy: make open-source alternatives easier to deploy at scale, starting with document collaboration and messaging across organisations and countries. Partners will use focused 100-Day Challenges, targeted development campaigns, to address barriers and deliver reusable improvements. Shared components, open interfaces and tested deployment methods will support a diverse European market, while preserving users’ ability to change tools or providers.

 Maintain: identify critical open-source dependencies and support their security, maintenance and continuity. An EU Sovereign Tech Fund pilot, led by the Sovereign Tech Agency within the Digital Commons EDIC, will commission maintenance of essential open-source components, testing a coordinated investment model across participating countries. Its findings will guide future support for the maintainers behind infrastructure that public services and businesses rely on.

 New Member State and Observers

 The Assembly approved Poland’s accession as a full Member State, effective 1 January 2027.

 The Digital Commons EDIC welcomes Croatia and Norway as new Observers.

 These admissions extend cooperation across Europe, creating further opportunities to communicate national needs and share expertise and deployment experience.

 Countries interested in participating can contact the Digital Commons EDIC to discuss the routes available under its Statutes.

 Advisory Board

 The Assembly appointed the Digital Commons EDIC’s eleven-member Advisory Board following an open call for experts. The Board will provide strategic and technical advice on adoption barriers, sustainable support models and priorities for shared investment.

 Its members bring expertise in software deployment and design, security, communities, maintenance, law and governance, technological autonomy, open-source AI, financing and entrepreneurship.

 The Digital Commons EDIC thanks all applicants and the organisations that supported their nominations.

 About the Digital Commons EDIC

 The Digital Commons EDIC is a European Digital Infrastructure Consortium bringing countries together to develop, maintain and scale digital commons. It works with public administrations, businesses and open-source communities to strengthen Europe’s capacity to use, adapt and sustain shared digital tools and infrastructure.

 Funding from the European Union’s Digital Europe Programme supports community activities, access to expertise, 100-Day Challenges, dependency assessments and the EU Sovereign Tech Fund maintenance pilot, led by the Sovereign Tech Agency within the Digital Commons EDIC. Countries and partners can contribute further expertise and resources.
